    Compounds

    Ionic Compounds
    Metal + nonmetal (usually solid).
    High melting point (Tm)
    Conducts electricity when dissolved in solute.
    Are neutral due to electrostatic attraction of cations and anions.
    Dissolve in water into their individual ions.
    Cation
    Positively charged
    Anion
    Negatively charged.
    Molecular Compounds
    Nonmetal + Nonmetal
    Solids, liquids, or gasses.
    Melting point (Tm) varies.
    Usually not good conductors.
    Diatomic Molecules
    H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂
    Exist naturally in a pair bonded to themselves.
    When we show chemical equations, always use the diatomic form!

    Cl₂ + H₂ = HCL

    Nomenclature

    Ionic
    Write the cation then the anion.
    For cations, use the same name as the element:
    • Use roman numerals to distinguish the positive charge of the ion.

    • Mg2+ = Magnesium

    • Cu+ = Copper (I)

    • Cu2+ = Copper (II)

    For anions, add ide to the root atom it comes from:
    • Don't include subscripts in the full name!
    Don't include greek prefixes for the number of atoms!
    Molecular
    If looking at the structural formula, central atoms come first.
    • Otherwise, use left to right order or lowest to highest atomic number.
    Add ide to the second component
    Use greek prefixes for the number of atoms
    • Leave off the greek prefixes for the first component if is mono.

    CO2 = Carbon Dioxide

    SO3 = Sulfur Trioxide

    H2) = DiHydrogen Monoxide

    Atomic Masses

    • Ratio of H to O is 1:16
    • 1g of H and 16g of O have the same number of atoms between them.
    • 1g of H to 21g of O, O has twice the number of atoms as the H.
    • Mass of H2O = mass (H) + mass (H) + mass (O).

    Avogadro's Number

    _NA = 6.022 x 10^23 particles/mole

    Mole
    The amount of substance that contains on NA of particles.
    Molar Mass
    1 amu = 1 g/mol

    • Mass on periodic table in amu tells us how many grams of an element are in 1 mole.

    • If we have 16g O, we have 1 mole, or 6.022 x 10^23 particles.

    • N = n + NA

      • N = number of particles
      • n = number of moles
      • n = m/MM
        • m = mass
        • MM = molar mass

    Conversion Factors

    There are 6.022E23 particles in 1 mole.

    How many atoms of carbon are in 10 g of carbon?

    • 10 g carbon / 12 g/mol (molas mass, MM) * 6.022E23 atoms/mol = 5E23 atoms C

    Chemical formulas can be used as conversion factors, shows ratios.

    1 mole O / 1 mole H2O 2 mole H / 1 mole H2O 2 mole H / 1 mole O

    Molar concentration is used for solutions
    Molarity
    M = mol/L. for liquids and gasses.
    Solute
    The dissolved substance
    Solvent
    The substance doing the dissolving.
    Solution
    Solute + solvent

    The Ideal Gas Law

    STP
    Standard temperature and pressure
    273.15K & 1 atm
    The Ideal Gas Law
    PV = nRT
    • P = pressure (atm)
    • V = volume (L)
    • n = moles of gas
    • T = temp (K)
    • R = gas constant (0.08206)
    Applies to all gasses.
    Limitations due to particle interactions at high pressure.
    Predict the final volume of 10L bloon if temp is 3x and P is 2x.

    1P * 10L = nR * 1T

    • 10 / 1 2P * 10L = nR * 3T
    • 20 / 3

    Proportions

    Directly Proportional
    Denoted with little alpha α.
    Opposite sides of the equals.
    As one increases, so does the other.
    Inversely Proportional
    As one increases, the other decreases.
    Same side as the equals.

    Laws

    Gay-Lussac's Law
    P α T
    Boyle's Law
    P α 1/V
    Charles Law
    V α T
    Dalton's Law of Partial Pressure
    Total pressure of a mixture of gasses is equal to the sum of pressures of individual gasses.
    Ptotal = P1 + P2 + P3
    Mole Fraction (X)
    Moles of each gas is ratios of partial to whole.

    Moles of individual / total moles in mixture.

    Pressure of individual / total pressure in mixture.
