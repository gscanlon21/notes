    Lab Notebook Structure

    Laboratory Notebook

    Laboratory Notebook is an extension of your brain - name & year on cover.

    EVERYTHING should be recorded here

    • leave 5 pages for table of contents (TOC); number all pages
    • each experiment title page - title, purpose, date!
    • hypothesis & prediction if needed,
    • math/formulas used,
    • data collected (tables), data analysis (calculations or graphs)
    • any observations, including weird ones,
    • ideas, relevant sketches
    • conclusions, next steps

    MUST be brought to each lab session – points deducted if not. Never take notes on loose-leaf paper, hand, paper towel, sticky notes, benchtop. WRITE CLEARLY. Follow the format below with HEADINGS

    Table of Contents TOC

    Date - Experiment Name.............................. page numbers

    TITLE

    write as shown on exercise. Complete title on 1st page only.

    PURPOSE

    write as given

    METHOD

    do NOT rewrite handout, write: "refer to lab exercise 'title and year'"

    • Bullet point the brief steps - check off each step once completed. Did math? Show formula!

    TROUBLESHOOTING

    what went wrong or sideways, and how did you work around? Leave room here to describe any mistakes made, or deviations from exercise protocol. It's okay, mistakes happen!

    NOTES

    if I write it on the board, you should be taking notes in your lab notebook. Relevant drawings are useful. Include odd/cool observations about the experiment; they may not make sense now, but may later, in retrospect.

    RESULTS

    here is where ALL data collected are documented, examples: chromosome drawings, penny flips, dice rolls, corn kernels counted, punnett squares, tables from exercise to complete. Did math? Show your work! If you create something in PPT or Excel, or snap a Photo - PRINT IT & tape it in notebook

    CONCLUSION

    answer questions in exercise, explain results, refer to your data in your answers, if there's a hypothesis: reject or fail-to-reject it.

    DO NOT WRITE OUT A 'ROUGH DRAFT' OF LAB NOTES on other paper, with the intention to transfer it all into a 'PERFECT' lab notebook. Nooooooo... This can get you fired in the biotech industry labs and clinical medicine. It always creates mistakes. There is often a subconscious bias introduced that misrepresents the procedure or changes the data. Mistakes: they happen!

    • do NOT use whiteout, do NOT blackout with sharpie, do NOT scribble out, do NOT rip out pages with mistakes
    • Do draw a simple "X" or line through the mistake and make the correction. "Correction on next page." You might go back later and find the ‘mistake’ was correct.

    LAB REPORT / WRITE UP

    due the week after the experiment is completely done.

    • Questions posed throughout the exercise – answer always in the lab notebook! Questions at end of lab protocol – don't forget to complete them! Use PQA = part question part answer to complete sentences.
    • Reference your notes, your observations, your data when discussing results.
    • Write up must be in lab journal in your handwriting. Write in legibly, handwriting must be readable. If you need accommodations, talk to me and I can oblige. :)
    • If using a photo, excel graph, or something from elsewhere: TAPE/STAPLE it INTO THE JOURNAL.

    SUBMIT REPORT

    ALL Lab Notebook pages from Title to Write Up.

    • Upload photo(s) of all material in Canvas Assignment for that experiment.
    • ABSOLUTELY NO emailed class assignments or submission: use Canvas upload

    PRE-LAB

    • Read lab slides and exercise to prepare lab journal before arrival.
    • Maybe QUIZ covering last week's lab or covering this week's pre-lab.
    • QUIZ might be a check of your lab journal to be sure you prepared the TOC, page numbers, tables from exercises, or to check that you wrote out your hypothesis / prediction, that you recorded data, did your PUNNETT SQUARES... etc.

    Week 1 Notes

    Homozygote
    AA or aa
    Heterozygote
    Aa

    n indicates the number of genome copies.

    Cohesins
    Keep sister chromatids together.
    Ploidy
    The number of complete sets of chromosomes in a cell.
    Aneuploidy
    An abnormal number of sets of chromosomes.
    • Due to mitotic nondisjunction (spindle fiber/anaphase error).
    • Trisomy, Monosomy, Nullisomy, Tetrasomy.
