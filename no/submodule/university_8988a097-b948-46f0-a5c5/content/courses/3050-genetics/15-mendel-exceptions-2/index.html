    DNA Methylation

    DNA Methylation
    Occurs near promoter control region.
    $$+CH_3$$ = gene off
    $$-CH_3$$ = gene on
    $$+CH_3$$ attaches to C (cytosine).
    When DNA repair occurs after replication, which strand is the mutation and which is the wildtype?
    • The unmethylated strand of the hemi-methylated double helix is identified as the mutant strand.
    • Then dna replication completes by putting a methyl group on the unmethylated strand.
    Cytosine Methylation Can Lead to Mutations
    Deamination
    Water deaminates $$NH_3$$ of methylated cytosine molecules and turns them into a thymine which doesn't belong in DNA.
    • The cell recognizes that a new T molecule does not belong with the opposite G molecule (originally C-G).
    • The cell doesn't know which is correct, may change G to an A, or may change T back into a C.

    Mismatch Repair

    Water deaminates $$NH_3$$ of unmethylated cytosine molecules and turns them into a uracil which doesn't belong in DNA.
    • This is a recognized mutation within the cell and dna repair will correct the uracil back into a cytosine.

    Recognized Abnormal Mutation

    CpG Islands

    CpG Islands
    Clusters of C-G base pairs.
    Where methylation happens, due to C bonding with $$CH_3$$
    Often acts as promoter regions for genes.

    Bent arrow denotes transcription start site.

    CpG Island Promoters
    Able to be methylated enough to affect gene expression.

    Seen affecting mostly transient genes (during development or for differentiated cells).

    Non-CpG Island Promoters
    Consistent, rare methylation attached to cytosine molecules in gene for replication purposes.
    Not enough methylation to affect gene expression.
    Expression is stable over time.

    Seen affecting mainly housekeeping genes that every cell needs.

    RNA-Based X-Inactivation

    RNA-Based X-Inactivation

    XIST non-coding RNA affects which chromosome turns into a barr body.

    XIST gene exists in x chromosome.
    • If methylated, the gene is inactivated.
    • If unmethylated, the gene is expressed, coats x chromosome and turns it into the barr body.
      • Called Lyonization.

    Genome-Wide Methyl Patterns

    Genome-Wide Methyl Patterns

    Imprinting patterns & disorders

    Epigenetic Imprints

    If an allele is methylated on one parental chromosome, and unmethylated on the other, only one allele is expressed which affects phenotypes of variants.

    This only affects somatic cells with 2 homologous chromosomes.

    In Sperm
    There is a male-specific methylation pattern across all 23 chromosomes.
    In Eggs
    There is a female-specific methylation pattern across all 23 chromosomes.
    • Your somatic cells all have the male imprint and female imprint on their respective parental chromosomes.
    • The methylation pattern is reset during meiosis to the parent's sex specific methylation pattern.

    Imprinting Patterns

    Autosome Epigenetic Imprinting of One Allele

    • Gene's affected by imprints likely have CpG Island Promoters that control gene expression.
    Parent of Origin
    The expression of imprinted genes in an offspring is determined by the parental imprint.
    Female Imprint
    Alleles from the mother are turned off.
    Male Imprint
    Alleles from the father are turned off.

    Additive Genes

    In a dihybrid cross of an additive model, you have 5 phenotypic classes in a ratio of 1:4:6:4:1.

    Number of Phenotypic Classes Additive Genes
    1 plus the number of dominant alleles.

    2 genes (2 alleles each) = 4 alleles = 0,1,2,3,4 = 5 phenotypes.

    • If dominant allele of multiple genes contributes 3 inches to plant height, genotypes with more dominant alleles have higher plants.

    Pascal's triangle reveals how many genotypes fall into each category.

    Number of Dominant Alleles

    Dominant alleles all have the same additive weight, A or B does not matter, just whether it is dominant or recessive.

    • $$C(x + y)^A$$
    • x is recessive
    • y is dominant
    • A is the total number of alleles in the system, twice the number of genes.
    • C (coefficient) reflects the number of genotypes in each bin.
