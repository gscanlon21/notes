    Terminology

    Gene
    A gene is a portion of the genome that codes for a specific protein.
    A diploid individual has two copies of every gene.
    Locus
    The locus is the genetic location of a gene.
    Allele
    An allele is a variation of a gene.
    A diploid individual has two alleles of a gene.
    Trait
    A trait is how the gene is expressed.
    True-Breeding
    The genotype over each generation is consistent and true.
    There's no variation in the genotype over each generation.
    Parental Strain
    The original genotypes of the parents.

    Overview

    1. Who was Gregor Mendel?
      • A hungarian botanist who first discovered independent assortment and recombination using quantitative methods.

    3. What does a 'selfing' or self-cross mean, in plants?
      • Selfing is the act of crossing a monoecious plant with itself.

    6. What is the difference between phenotype and genotype?
      • Genotype is the combination of alleles received from the parents.
      • Phenotype is the expressed trait derived from the combination of alleles.
    7. What is the principle of dominance? Why are there more genotypes than phenotypes?
      • Dominance causes certain alleles to overwrite other alleles. This makes it so some combinations of alleles don't matter if the dominant allele exists.
    8. What is the principle of allele segregation? When does this happen in the M-phase (what sub-phase)?

    9. What is the principle of independent assortment? When does that actually happen in the the M-phase (what sub-phase)? This refers to 2 or more genes - what types of genes does this apply.

    11. What ratio was observed in the F2 generation in Mendel's monohybrid crosses?
      • ?
    12. What is the definition of Dominance according to Mendel? What type of individual shows this?
      • ?
    13. What is the definition of recessive according to Mendel? What type of individual shows this?
      • ?
    14. Why do we say that the allele is dominant or recessive and not the gene?
      • Because the allele is a variation of the gene. The gene always exists, what shape it takes is controlled by the allele.
    15. Why can we use the multiplication rule to predict the outcome of dihybrid crosses? What is the assumption made for this to be true?
      • The assumption is that these are independent events. We can use the multiplication rule because the probabilities of two independent events are the product of their individual probabilities.
    16. What is a test cross? What can it determine? Are you testing an organism with a dominant or recessive phenotype? What are you testing for? What is the phenotype of the organism you bring into the cross to do the test ← the "tester strain"?
      • A test cross tells you if the genotype of the dominant phenotype you are looking at is heterozygous or homozygous dominant.
      • You are crossing your unknown genotype with a homozygous recessive strain.
    17. What is a reciprocal cross? What is the expected outcome?
      • A reciprocal cross takes two phenotypes and crosses them together twice, one with the sexes of the phenotypes flipped.
      • If the outcome has no sex skew, that indicates the genes for the phenotype exist on the autosomal chromosome.
      • If the outcome has a sex skew, that indicates the genes for the phenotype exist on a sex chromosome.

    Formation of Gametes

    1. Kelp, a large, multicellular type of algae, has air bladders in the blades to help them float near the surface of the ocean where they can get more sunlight. Assume that having many air bladders (k) is a recessive trait. What gametes would be produced by a heterozygous individual?
      • Kk, Kk
    2. In humans, sickle cell anemia is a recessive disease caused by a mutation of a single gene or 'locus' which codes for an important blood protein. The allele for the normal protein (S) is dominant to that for the one causing sickle cell anemia, s. What gametes would be produced by an individual who suffers from sickle cell anemia?
      • ss, ss
    3. In areas of the very deep and damp southeastern United States lives what is essentially a giant flying cockroach known as a Palmetto bug. Assume that long antennae (L) are dominant to short antennae and that normal wings (N) are dominant to bent ones. What gametes will be produced by an individual that is homozygous dominant for antennae and heterozygous for wings?
      • LN, LN, Ln, Ln
    4. Suppose you are designing a new Jurassic Park and you would like to have the crowd drawing presence of Velociraptor without the danger of having your crowds eaten. You have managed to come up with two varieties, very aggressive (A), which is apparently a dominant trait in Velociraptors, and non-aggressive. They also come in two colors, red (R), which is dominant, and green. At the moment you have a green female that is homozygous dominant for aggression and a non-aggressive male that is homozygous dominant for color. What gametes will be produced by the male and female?
      • Female: Ar, Ar, Ar, Ar
      • Male: aR, aR, aR, aR
