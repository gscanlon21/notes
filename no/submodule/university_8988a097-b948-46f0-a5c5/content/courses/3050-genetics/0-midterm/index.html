    Binomial Probability
    $$P = \binom{n}{r} p^r (1-p)^{n-r}$$
    Use the nCr button to calculate $$\binom{n}{r}$$
    • $$n = 3$$ (total events)
    • $$r = 1$$ (target events)
    • $$p = \frac{1}{4}$$ (probability of event)
    • $$1-p = \frac{3}{4}$$ (probability of no event)

    Terminology

    Gene
    A gene is a portion of the genome that codes for a specific protein.
    A diploid individual has two copies of every gene.
    Locus
    The locus is the genetic location of a gene.
    Allele
    An allele is a variation of a gene.
    A diploid individual has two alleles of a gene.
    Trait
    A trait is how the gene is expressed.
    True-Breeding
    The genotype over each generation is consistent and true.
    There's no variation in the genotype over each generation.
    Parental Strain
    The original genotypes of the parents.
    Hybrid
    A cross between two individuals or strains.
    Dominant
    Dominant alleles mask the recessive allele and produce the observed trait in the offspring.
    Recessive
    Recessive alleles are hidden unless homozygous and can skip generations.
    Monohybrid
    A cross between two individuals with one observed trait.
    Dihybrid
    A cross between two individuals with two observed traits.
    P Generation
    The parental generation
    F₁ Generation
    The first generation.
    F₂ Generation
    The second generation.
    Test Cross
    Use a dominant hetero or homozygote and cross them with a homozygous recessive mate
    • Tells if the parent is homozygous or heterozygous dominant trait.
    Reciprocal Cross
    Is this gene on autosomal or sex chromosomes?
    Must switch male/female phenotypes in second cross.
    Cross Heterozygote
    A heterozygote cross produces a 1:2:1 genotypic ratio and 3:1 phenotypic ratio from the parental genotypes of Aa and Aa.
    Punnett Square
    A visualization of a genetic cross between two individuals showing their respective offspring genotype and phenotype probabilities.
    Monohybrid Punnett Square
    A small 2x2 punnett square for 2 gametes from each parent.
    Dihybrid Punnett Square
    A large 4x4 punnett square for 4 gametes from each parent.
    Dihybrid Punnett Squares
    Make two small 2x2 punnett squares and multiply their respective probabilities.
    Dihybrid Phenotype Branching Diagram
    Multiply the probabilities of phenotypes from each respective independent event.
    Dihybrid Genotype Branching Diagram
    Multiply the probabilities of genotypes from each respective independent event.
    Autosomal Dominant Inheritance
    One dominant allele is all that's needed to be inherited for the trait for be expressed.

    Example

    Autosomal Recessive Inheritance
    Two recessive alleles must be inherited for the trait for be expressed.

    Example

    Tumor Suppressors Inheritance
    At a cellular level: according to Hudsen's Two-Hit Hypothesis, two mutations are necessary for suppressed tumor suppressor function.
    At an organismal level: one variant allele when passed on creates a genetic predisposition to cancer, may be dominant.
    Chromosome Numbers, arms p/q
    Chromosomes are numbered by size descending.
    The p-arm is the short arm.
    The q-arm is the long arm.

    Autosome
    An autosome is a normal-type, non-sex, chromosome.

    Dosage Compensation
    Individuals with multiple copies of a sex chromosome will see one inactivate in order to normalize the expression of those genes.
    Barr Body
    An inactivated X-chromosome.
    At 15 days gestation, individuals with two copies of the X-chromosome will see one randomly inactivate within each cell in order to normalize gene transcription, turning it into a barr body.
    X-Linked
    Genes that are linked to the X-chromosome.
    Y-Linked
    Genes that are linked to the Y-chromosome.
    Hemizygous
    Describes a diploid organisms that only has 1 copy of a gene or chromosome when there are normally 2 copies.
    Holandric Inheritance
    Refers to genes that are linked to the Y-chromosome.
    Female "Carrier"
    A female carrier is a female who carries a disease allele on one of their two X chromosomes.
    Sperm Storage (in fly females)
    Female flies are able to store male sperm for several days and can choose when to fertilize an egg.
    Random X-Inactivation
    At 15 days gestation or 5000 cells, a random X chromosome in each cell will inactivate into a barr body in order to normalize the total expression of X-linked genes.

    General

    1. What are the three traditional disciplines in the study of Genetics?

    2. Name 3 model organisms used in Genetics, and what type of biology are they used to model.
      • Drosophilia (fruit flies) can model alzheimers and diabetes.
      • C. elegans can model parkinsons and diabetes.
      • Ants can model evolutionary genetics through the homologous HOX genes.
    3. Name 3 differences in the DNA of prokaryotes vs. eukaryote cells.
      • Prokaryotic DNA is a double stranded circle, don't have histones, have plasmids.
      • Prokaryotic DNA exists in the cytoplasm. Eukaryotic DNA exists in a protected nucleus.
    4. Why is DNA packing so important in eukaryotes?
      • Because each cell contains an unfathomably large amount of genetic material.
    5. What cells go through the Cell Cycle? (Eukaryotes or Prokaryotes?)
      • Eukaryotes go through the cell cycle, but not the mitochondria or chloroplasts.
    6. What does homology mean when we talk about genes? How do we determine if genes are homologous? What do we need to compare?
      • Homologous means the genes are derived from a shared ancestry, I assume we look at the locus of a gene.
    7. What is the normal time it takes for a cell to complete one cell cycle? Is there a universal "normal" time?
      • There is not a universal normal time. Depending on the organism, it varies, within or exceeding the range of 8 min to 1 year.
    8. What is the name of the stage of the cell cycle that deals specifically with nuclear division in growing cell populations?
      • Telophase in the M phase of the cell cycle.

      • Because each parent passes on a different chromosome to each offspring at a different ratio.
      • Females pass on their X-chromosome 50% of the time to both males and female offspring.
