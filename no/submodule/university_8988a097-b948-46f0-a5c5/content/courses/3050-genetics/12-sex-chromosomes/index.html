    Tips

    • Write down alleles
    • Genotype of parents
    • Phenotype of parents
    • Gametes (haploid)

    Y-Chromosome has $$\frac{1}{3}$$ of the genetic material as the X-chromosome.

    Sex-linked genes don't matter if dominant or recessive if person only has 1 copy of the gene.

    Sex-Linked Genes

    Male
    Transmits X to all daughters.
    Transmits Y to all sons.
    Female
    Can be carriers of recessive alleles
    Transmits 1 X randomly to son or daughter.

    X-Linked Recessive

    (XLR)

    • Disproportionaly affects males
    • Traits appear dominant in males
    • Traits appear recessive in females - more often carriers.

    • Red/green colorblindness

    • Hemophilia

    • Fragile-X Syndrome

    Pedigrees

    • Male = square
    • Female = circle

    X-Linked Dominant

    (XLD)

    • Phenotype is observed in heterozygous females
    • Affected males produce all affected daughters, but not affected sons.
    • Disproportiontly affects females

    • Rickets

    Y-Linked Traits

    Y-Traits
    Appear only in males.
    Every male affects every offspring male (but not if cheating parents).

    Hearing loss

    X-Inactivation

    X-Inactivation $$X_i$$
    Occurs in females at 15 days gestation.
    Mosaic of which X is active and which is inactive.
    Affects severity of dominant X.
    Complicates X-Linked Recessive (XLR) traits.
    $$X^+X^i$$ females can be colorblind
    • Called a manifesting heterozygote.

    Female Tortoise-Shell Cats

    Barr Body
    Condensed X = inactive X
    Males do not have a barr body.

    Dosage Compensation

    X-Dosage Compensation
    Females - in each cell - 1 x is inactive
    At 15 days gestation or 5000 cells, a female fetus has all their cells have a random x-chromosome inactivate. Creates a mosaic of cell function.
    Inactive x is called a barr-body.
    All future descendants of cells have the same x-chromosome inactivated.
    Klinefelter Syndrome
    An XXY male has $$XX_iY genome$$
    How is there a phenotype at all?
    • Some genes escape x-inactivation, some genes are expressed from both X chromosomes.
    Turner Syndrome
    XO genome, only 1 X
    Why is there a phenotype at all?
    • Some genes escape X-inactivation and are expressed from both chromosomes.

    Dosage Compensation Strategies

    • Used to equalize x-expression in mammals. Mammals : Random inactivation of 1 x chromosome.
    Fruit Flies
    Have double the transcription rate when there is only 1 X chromosome in males.
    Worms
    Both X-chromosomes are repressed by half.

    Sex <> Gender

    • SRY translocation
    • Androgen insensitivity.
