    • A and B alleles are complete dominant over the O allele.
    • A and B alleles are co-dominant with each other - both express.

    Lethal Alleles

    Lethal Alleles
    Lethal alleles are recessive because you can never get two homozygous offspring to cross.
    Changes offspring ratios because some offspring is unviable.
    Offspring Ratios
    2/3 chance heterozygote
    1/3 chance homozygote recessive
    Achondroplasia
    A is dominant for dwarfism.
    A is recessive for lethality.
    a is recessive for wildtype.
    AA
    lethal
    Aa
    dwarf
    aa
    wildtype

    Epistasis

    This is an extension to Mendelian genetics.

    Epistasis
    Two genes that independently produce unique phenotypes but together produce the phenotype of only one gene.
    Epistatic
    The dominant gene when two phenotypes interact and one takes precedence.
    Hypostatic
    The non-dominant gene when two phenotypes interact and one takes precedence.

    Recessive Epistasis

    Recessive Epistasis
    Recessive gene hides the phenotype of the other gene.
    bb hides A_ and a_
    • Gene B is epistatic to gene A.
    Turns a heterozygous 9:3:3:1 phenotypic cross into a 9:3:4 phenotypic class.

    Pigment gene doesn't matter if there is another gene that controls whether pigment is deposited or not.

    • Albino Pigmentation

    • Recessive albino gene controls whether pigmentation is deposited or not.

    • Bombay Blood Type

    • Recessive H gene is epistatic to gene I (ABO alleles) and prevents sugar tether - mimics blood type O.

    Dominant Negative Epistasis

    Dominant Negative Epistasis
    A dominant mutation that knocks out 1 enzyme and a recessive enzyme that knocks out another enzyme.
    F2 phenotypic ratio = 12:3:1

    Double Recessive Epistasis

    Double Recessive Epistasis
    F2 phenotypic ratio = 9:7

    Need two dominant enzymes to both work in order to express phenotype.

    Recessive alleles knock-out enzymes.

    This is due to Complementary Gene Action.

    Complementation

    Complementation
    When there are two different genes involved in a phenotype, and both genes are mutated, offspring results in wildtype.
    Parents have mutated phenotypes.
    All offspring are wildtype.

    • 2 Mutant parents (P)

    • 1 Wildtype offspring (F1)

    Non-Complementation

    Non-Complementation
    Two different mutations in the same gene cannot be involved in a complement.
    All offspring are mutated.

    Sex Traits

    Sex-Limited Traits
    Autosomally encoded genes.
    Requires hormones for expression.
    Usually only expressed in either males or females.

    Lactation

    Sex-Influenced Traits
    Autosomally encoded genes.
    Expressed in both sexes, though dominant in one sex and recessive in the other.

    Human baldness, male requires A_ while female requires aa.

    • Ratios are flipped between sexes.
