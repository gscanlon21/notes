    DNA Synthesis

    • DNA is synthesized in the 5' to 3' direction.
    • DNA is antiparallel.

    When a nucleotide triphosphate gets added to the 3' end of dna, two phosphates are detached and release energy.

    • Only one phosphate is used to attach to the 3'-OH hydroxyl.

    DNA Structure

    Rosalind Franklin
    Used x-ray crystallography to deduce the structure of DNA.
    Antiparallel DNA Structure
    Each strand is the reverse complement of one another.
    The 5'-Phosphate end and the 3'-OH hydroxyl end are not identical, they have different functions.

    Why do the Bases Pair the way they do?

    • Hydrogen bonds between bases are linear
    • The orientation of the bases align perfectly with the bases they match with.

    Complimentary Base Pairing

    A-T Base Pairs
    Have two hydrogen bonds.
    G-C Base Pairs
    Have three hydrogen bonds.

    Base Pairs

    Base Pair bp
    A unit of measure for DNA fragments.
    Motif
    Specific signature sequence.
    Usually a 12-bp fragment of DNA.
    kilobase kb
    1,000 base pairs.

    Reading DNA

    DNA is always read in the 5' to 3' direction, same as it's synthesized. Both the top and bottom strand are read in the same direction.

    DNA Fragments

    (dsDNA)

    <5% of all RNA in the cell is mRNA

    5'-ATGAGCATTCG-3'
    Top strand (+)
    Forward strand
    Coding strand
    Sense strand
    • Relative to coding
    3'-TACTCGTAAGGC-5'
    Bottom strand (-)
    Reverse strand
    Template strand
    Antisense strand
    • Relative to sense

    RNA polymerase enzyme attaches to the bottom (template) strand and reads the bottom strand to synthesize RNA that looks like the top (coding) strand. RNA polymerase also synthesizes new RNA in the 5' to 3' direction.

    Telomere Structures

    • Ends of DNA are subject to degredation due to the reactive nature of the hydroxyl and phosphate.
    • At the telomeres, a 3' overhand of a repetitive motif overhang's the 5' end and loops back in on itself (t-loop) to attach snugly in the helix.
    3' Overhang
    Degrades easily.
    T-Looping
    The overhang loops back and attaches to the helix to protect it.

    Organelle DNA

    • Inheritance mostly from female egg/ovule.

    DNA Replication

    This applies to nuclear (linear) genomes. For organelle genomes, DNA is circular, and there is only one origin and little repair.

    Replication
    DNA uses semi-conservative replication where the original strand is split and replicated with new material.
    Origins of Replication
    DNA is too large to replicate in a synchronous fashion.
    Has a DNA motif that tells proteins to start here.

      • At each origin, DNA helicases break apart the weak hydrogen bonds between base pairs and create a replication bubble.

      • The bubble expands out in both directions for each complementary strand.

      • The replication bubbles meet and combine.

      • After replication, cell cycle checkpoint looks for mistakes.

    Enzymes

    Helicase
    Breaks hydrogen bonds between base pairs.
    Single-Strand Binding Proteins
    Helps keep DNA from reconnecting.
    Gyrase
    Relieves torsional stress from the dsDNA that's waiting to unwind.

    Polymerase Chain Reaction

    (PCR)

    Overview
    DNA Replication in a test tube.
    Creates a short strand
    Melting Temperature
    Melting temp for primer is based on its sequence.
    Steps
    1. Denature
    2. Anneal
    3. Extend

    Steps

    Denature
    Heating DNA to break dsDNA strands into two ssDNA strands.
    Anneal Binding
    Primers bind to one end of a target segment of a ssDNA to copy.
    • Primers are forward primer and reverse primer, each binds to one complementary DNA.
    • Define amplicon size.
    Extend Synthesize
    DNA polymerase uses building blocks in the solution to to create a copy of the target segment.
