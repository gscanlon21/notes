# Cofactors

> [!NOTE]
> Many cofactors of B12 are also critical parts of the Krebs cycle.

**Biotin (B7)**
: Used to convert acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA, and is responsible for donating acetyl groups to the Krebs cycle. This is used for fatty acid synthesis.
: [Biotin is used to metabolise several carboxylase enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotin#Cofactor_biochemistry), at least 4 of which (ACC1, ACC2, PC, PCC) are involved in fatty acid metabolism. We saw earlier that the fatty acids are used for myelin synthesis.

**Thiamine (B1)**
: [Used for a lot of things, but the most-understood one seems to be catabolisim of sugars and amino acids.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiamine#Biological_functions)
: The most-understood thiamine derivative is thiamine pyrophosphate, which among other things is used as a coenzyme of [pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyruvate_dehydrogenase). [PDH is used to convert pyruvate to acetyl-CoA, which is then used in the Krebs cycle.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyruvate_dehydrogenase)
: Thamine pyrophosphate is also used as a coenzyme of 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase (OGDH) to convert [2-oxoglutarate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-Ketoglutaric_acid) to [succinyl-CoA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succinyl-CoA) during the Krebs cycle.
: [PDH and OGDH are used to synthesise myelin and acetylcholine.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiamine#Thiamine_pyrophosphate)

**Riboflavin (B2)**
: [Used for forming 2 coenzymes, FMN and FAD.](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/)
: These enzymes are then used for [metabolism of B3, B6, and B9](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Riboflavin-HealthProfessional/).
: Riboflavin also seems to affect iron metabolism, which can [help treat iron deficiency](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/public-health-nutrition/article/role-of-vitamins-in-the-prevention-and-control-of-anaemia/56893B7153231DAF42056C35739D9221).

**[Niacin (B3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_B3#Mechanism_of_action)**
: Used in the Krebs cycle.

**Pantothenic acid (B5)**
: [Used in the production of Coenzyme A (CoA)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0163782705000123)

**B6**
_Specifically as P5P_
: [Used in the synthesis of major neurotransmitters](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6071262/)
: - serotonin, dopamine, epinephrine, norepinephrine, GABA
: B6 is also used for the synthesis of sphingolipids, one of which is [sphingomyelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphingomyelin). Sphingomyelins are, as the name implies, an important part of the myelin sheath around nerves.

**Iron Metabolism**
: [Synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyrin#Biosynthesis) of [heme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heme) is affected by the Krebs cycle.

**[Lipoic Acid (LA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipoic_acid)**
: A part of the Krebs cycle as well, which may explain why some people see neuropathy-related benefit from it: [LA is used to produce acetyl-CoA](https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/lipoic-acid), which is then utilised by B7.


## Trace Minerals

**[Lithium](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07315724.2002.10719188)**
: [Lithium is used to transport B9 and B12 into the cells](https://www.jpands.org/vol20no4/marshall.pdf?crsi=662497726)

**[Molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum_in_biology)** 
: An important part of clearing sulfites from the blood.

**[Boron](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4712861/)**
: Important for both hormonal function and vitamin D function.
